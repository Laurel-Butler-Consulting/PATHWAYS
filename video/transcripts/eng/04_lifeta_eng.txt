So you want to be a teaching artist? That's awesome. A teaching artist is basically just a practicing artist who teaches. The trick is that a lot of people in society think you can just throw an artist in the classroom, and they can just kind of teach. Artists are amazing, artists can do anything, but having some training and skills like classroom management and lesson planning can really go a long way in terms of successful experiences for you and your future students.

There are a lot of training programs out there, some of which are associated with non-profits who then hire teaching artists and will then place you in schools, museums, community centers. It might be one-off workshops or longer-term residences in the classroom after school. The sky is really the limit in terms of what teaching artists can do. It's very helpful to have some arts integration training so that you can connect the arts with other academic subjects, and it's really helpful to have social emotional learning training so that your arts teaching can enrich the whole person.

It's also important to remember that being a teaching artist is often freelance gig work. You might even start your own little business, and even if you don't, you still really need to market your services and know what sets your teaching artistry apart, because it becomes the product. 

I love being a teaching artist because it's very independent, very dynamic. You can go from project to project, do multiple jobs at the same time, take breaks to focus on your own artwork. But you have to know that's the lifestyle you're signing up for. If this sounds like you, check out some of the training programs so that you can get started acquiring the skills and tools that you'll need to enter the exciting world of teaching artistry.
