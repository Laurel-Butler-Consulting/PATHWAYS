Every week I get at least three emails from districts all over the state of California looking for theater teachers. It's a great time to be a theater teacher. 

In order to get into a credential program, you need to meet the following requirements: first, you need a bachelor's degree; second, you need to show subject matter competency. Subject matter competency simply means that you know your stuff about theater. This is because a credential program doesn't teach you what to teach. It teaches you how to teach what you know. Most applicants already have an undergraduate or graduate theater degree, which is all you need for subject matter competency. 

Most people who don't have subject matter competency through a degree go through a process called transcript evaluation. This means that you complete a form, and somebody - at Cal State East Bay, me - looks at your transcripts and determines if you have the knowledge and skills to teach theater. This almost always fulfills subject matter competency, especially if you have a theater or a drama minor. As a last resort, if you don't have a degree, you can always take the CSET exam. 

Professional experience and classes taken outside of higher education do not count towards subject matter competency. And although many of our students have lots of experience working with kids, teaching experience is not a requirement to get into the program. Remember, it's our job to teach you how to teach. What is important is that you have the dispositions to be a good teacher, such as an understanding of collaboration, the habit of self-reflection, the belief that all students have the ability to learn, the willingness to take on responsibilities, and a commitment to ongoing learning.
