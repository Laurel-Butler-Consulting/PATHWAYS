CTE stands for Career Technical Education, and it differs from a traditional academic teaching credential in several ways. A CTE credential authorizes you to teach within a specific subject area, because you have a depth and breadth of experience as a working professional in that field. The courses that you would then be eligible to teach with the CTE would be designated as career, technical, vocational or trade courses by the district in which you get hired.

Some basic requirements to earn a CTE credential in arts media entertainment include having a high school diploma and verification from employers that you have at least three years of work experience directly related to arts, media, and entertainment. That's at least 1000 hours each year for three years. What's unique about this credential is you don't have to enroll in a multi-year university program.

You can pursue it through your county office of education, university extensions, and other continuing education programs with a preliminary CTE. You can even work towards your credential while you're teaching in a classroom. And typically, those with a CTE credential continue to pursue their arts practice and stay active in their creative field. There are other details outlined in this website, but the most important point to remember is that your experience, your knowledge, insight, and skills from working in the field of arts, media, and entertainment are requirements for earning that CTE credential, and they're also what distinguish you and make you eligible to teach.
